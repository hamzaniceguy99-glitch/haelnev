How we work

Why we put the limit first

Patients often arrive hoping the physiotherapist will finally tell them what is wrong. It is an understandable hope, and it is one a practitioner can quietly satisfy with a confident-sounding explanation. But establishing a medical diagnosis is reserved to physicians in Belgium, and a physiotherapist who blurs that line is not being helpful — they are standing between a patient and an examination that may matter.

The honest version is narrower and more useful. We can test how a joint moves, measure the range, find the load at which a complaint appears, and recognise when a pattern does not behave like a musculoskeletal problem at all. That last part is the one that protects people: night pain that does not settle, weakness that spreads, a complaint that worsens regardless of load. Those go back to a doctor the same week, not after another block of sessions.

The same honesty applies to outcomes. Recovery depends on the condition, on how a body responds and on what happens in the days between appointments, which is why we prescribe three exercises rather than twelve and ask whether they actually fit your week. A practice that promises a number of sessions and a cure is selling certainty that nobody examining a body can truthfully offer.
